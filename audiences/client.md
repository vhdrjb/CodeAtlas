# Audience: Client

**Perspective**: External stakeholder who has commissioned or is evaluating the project for business purposes.

---

## Profile

Clients are non-technical or semi-technical stakeholders who need to understand what they are paying for, what they are getting, and whether the project meets their expectations. They evaluate projects through the lens of business value, reliability, and professionalism. A client reading Code Atlas output wants confidence that the project is well-built, well-managed, and aligned with their needs.

Common scenarios for clients using Code Atlas:

- **Evaluating a deliverable** from a development team or agency.
- **Reviewing progress** on a commissioned project.
- **Assessing technical quality** before accepting a handoff.
- **Preparing internal communication** about the project to their own stakeholders.

---

## Tone and Style

- **Professional and confidence-inspiring**. The output should make the client feel the project is in capable hands.
- **Value-focused**. Every technical detail should be translated into a business benefit.
- **Transparent but positive**. Be honest about the project's state, but frame everything constructively.
- **Accessible**. Assume the client has basic technical literacy but is not a developer.
- **Structured for navigation**. Clients may jump to sections that interest them — use clear headings.

---

## Terminology

- Use plain language with technical terms explained in context.
- Replace technical jargon with business-friendly alternatives where possible.
- When technical terms are necessary, follow them with a brief, non-technical explanation.
- Use the client's language, not the engineering team's language.

---

## Detail Level

- **Product capabilities**: Full detail — what the system does and what users experience.
- **Project status**: Full detail — what is delivered, what is in progress, what is planned.
- **Quality indicators**: Moderate detail — testing, documentation, and code quality presented as reliability signals.
- **Technology**: Brief — name the stack with a one-line business justification for each choice.
- **Architecture**: Brief — high-level design explained in terms of benefits (scalability, security, speed).
- **Implementation specifics**: None — clients should never see file paths, code snippets, or internal module details.

---

## What to Emphasize

- What the system does and the value it delivers.
- What has been delivered vs. what remains.
- Quality and reliability indicators.
- Security and data protection measures.
- Performance and scalability characteristics.
- Timeline and milestone status.

---

## What to De-emphasize

- Internal code structure and module organization.
- Individual dependency versions.
- Build and deployment internals.
- Technical debt (unless reframed as "identified improvement opportunities").
- Error handling and logging specifics.

---

## Recommended Analysis Levels

- **Standard**: Best for most client communications — thorough but accessible.
- **Quick**: Suitable for a brief status update or executive summary for the client.
- **Deep**: Useful for formal project handoff documentation.
