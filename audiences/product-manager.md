# Audience: Product Manager

**Perspective**: Business-oriented stakeholder who needs to understand what the product does, for whom, and where it is headed.

---

## Profile

Product Managers need to understand the intersection of user needs, business goals, and technical feasibility. They are not primarily interested in implementation details — they want to know what the product can do, what it should do next, and what constraints exist. PMs reading Code Atlas output are typically evaluating a project's fit with a roadmap, assessing feature completeness, or preparing stakeholder communications.

Common scenarios for PMs using Code Atlas:

- **Evaluating an existing product** for gaps against a target roadmap.
- **Preparing a product brief** or status report for leadership.
- **Onboarding** onto a new product team.
- **Communicating with engineering** about priorities and constraints.

---

## Tone and Style

- **Business-oriented but technically informed**. Use technical terms when they describe product capabilities ("REST API with OAuth 2.0"), but explain implications in business terms.
- **Feature-centric**. Organize information around what users can do, not how the code is structured.
- **Status-aware**. PMs need to know what exists vs. what is planned — clearly distinguish between them.
- **Actionable**. Every section should help the PM make a decision or communicate something.

---

## Terminology

- Use product and business terminology: user stories, features, epics, roadmap, MVP.
- Translate technical concepts into product impact: "PostgreSQL with JSONB columns" becomes "supports flexible data structures without schema changes."
- Avoid deep technical jargon unless it directly affects product decisions.
- When technical details are necessary, add a one-line product-impact explanation.

---

## Detail Level

- **Product features**: Full detail — what exists, what is planned, user value.
- **User experience**: Moderate detail — user flows, pain points, and opportunities.
- **Architecture**: Brief — pattern and tech stack, no implementation details.
- **Implementation status**: Full detail — completion, progress, and gaps.
- **Technical constraints**: Moderate detail — only those that affect product decisions.
- **Business context**: Moderate detail — domain, users, value proposition.

---

## What to Emphasize

- Feature set and completion status.
- User personas and target market.
- User flows and experience quality.
- Roadmap and planned features.
- Technical constraints that limit product possibilities.
- Competitive differentiators.

---

## What to De-emphasize

- Code-level implementation details.
- Internal module architecture.
- Build and deployment specifics.
- Specific dependency versions.
- Code quality metrics (unless they indicate reliability risks).

---

## Recommended Analysis Levels

- **Standard**: Best for most PM use cases — product and status focus without overwhelming detail.
- **Quick**: Suitable for a rapid product check or status summary.
- **Deep**: Useful when PM needs a thorough product audit or competitive analysis.
