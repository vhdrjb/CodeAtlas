# Audience: Recruiter

**Perspective**: Talent acquisition professional evaluating a candidate's project experience.

---

## Profile

Recruiters scan resumes and portfolios quickly — typically spending 6-10 seconds per entry. They look for specific signals: recognizable technologies, evidence of impact, scope of responsibility, and indicators of seniority. They are not technical experts, but they know enough to spot meaningful patterns.

Recruiters using Code Atlas are typically:

- **Internal recruiters** assessing a candidate's past project work before an interview.
- **Technical recruiters** evaluating whether a candidate's experience matches a job description.
- **Hiring managers** reviewing a candidate's portfolio alongside a recruiter.
- **Candidates themselves** generating professional descriptions of their projects.

---

## Tone and Style

- **Professional and polished**. Every sentence should read like it belongs on a resume or LinkedIn profile.
- **Achievement-oriented**. Focus on what was accomplished, not what was attempted.
- **Keyword-rich**. Include recognizable technology names for ATS systems and recruiter searches.
- **Quantified**. Use numbers wherever possible — team size, user count, performance improvements.
- **Concise**. Shorter is always better for recruiters.

---

## Terminology

- Use industry-standard job titles and role descriptions.
- Include framework and technology names exactly as they appear in job postings.
- Avoid obscure or project-specific terminology.
- Translate technical achievements into skills that appear in job descriptions: "implemented caching layer" becomes "experience with caching strategies and performance optimization."

---

## Detail Level

- **Technologies used**: Full detail — every recognizable technology is a keyword.
- **Scope and impact**: Full detail — scale, user count, business results.
- **Responsibility level**: Moderate detail — what the candidate likely owned.
- **Architecture**: Minimal — unless it demonstrates seniority (e.g., "designed microservices architecture").
- **Implementation details**: None — recruiters do not evaluate code quality.

---

## What to Emphasize

- Technologies that match common job requirements.
- Evidence of full-stack or end-to-end capability.
- Scale indicators (users, data, traffic).
- Complexity indicators (real-time, distributed, security).
- Business impact and measurable outcomes.
- Collaboration and team size.

---

## What to De-emphasize

- Internal project structure and module details.
- Testing practices.
- Build and deployment processes.
- Code quality assessments.
- Technical debt or known issues.
- Anything negative or incomplete.

---

## Recommended Analysis Levels

- **Quick**: Best for generating resume bullet points — fast and focused.
- **Standard**: Suitable for a portfolio description or detailed application.
- **Deep**: Rarely necessary — only for comprehensive portfolio pages.
