# Audience: Investor

**Perspective**: Financial stakeholder evaluating the project's market potential, team capability, and technical viability.

---

## Profile

Investors assess projects through the lens of risk, return, and competitive advantage. They want to understand whether the technology is defensible, whether the team can execute, and whether the product addresses a real market opportunity. They are less interested in how something works and more interested in whether it works well enough to win in the market.

Investors using Code Atlas are typically:

- **Venture capitalists** evaluating a startup's technical capabilities during due diligence.
- **Angel investors** assessing whether a founder's project demonstrates technical competence.
- **Corporate development teams** evaluating acquisition targets.
- **Grant reviewers** assessing the technical feasibility of a funded project.

---

## Tone and Style

- **Strategic and analytical**. Frame technical decisions in terms of competitive advantage and market positioning.
- **Evidence-based**. Support claims with specific observations from the codebase.
- **Balanced**. Acknowledge both strengths and risks — investors lose trust with overly optimistic assessments.
- **Business-fluent**. Translate every technical capability into a business implication.

---

## Terminology

- Use business and investment terminology: moat, scalability, defensibility, time-to-market, burn rate.
- Explain technical advantages in market terms: "uses event-driven architecture" becomes "designed for real-time processing, enabling competitive features like live collaboration."
- Avoid deep technical implementation details unless they demonstrate a proprietary advantage.

---

## Detail Level

- **Market positioning**: Moderate detail — what problem is solved and for whom.
- **Technical differentiation**: Full detail — what makes the technology unique or hard to replicate.
- **Scalability**: Full detail — can this grow to serve a large market?
- **Implementation maturity**: Moderate detail — is the product real or vaporware?
- **Team signals**: Moderate detail — coding standards, documentation, and process quality indicate team maturity.
- **Architecture specifics**: Brief — only when they demonstrate competitive advantage.

---

## What to Emphasize

- Unique technical capabilities that create competitive moats.
- Evidence of execution ability (code quality, testing, documentation).
- Scalability and growth potential of the architecture.
- Technology choices that reduce costs or accelerate development.
- Real, working features vs. planned features.
- Team maturity signals (coding standards, process quality).

---

## What to De-emphasize

- Routine implementation details.
- Internal module organization.
- Specific dependency versions.
- Build and deployment internals.
- Detailed code quality metrics.
- Known issues and technical debt (unless they represent material risk).

---

## Recommended Analysis Levels

- **Standard**: Best for investor due diligence — covers product, technology, and maturity.
- **Quick**: Suitable for an initial screening or summary.
- **Deep**: Appropriate for acquisition due diligence or major investment decisions.
