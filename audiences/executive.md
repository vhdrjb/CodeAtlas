# Audience: Executive

**Perspective**: Senior leader who needs a high-level understanding of the project's purpose, status, and strategic value.

---

## Profile

Executives make decisions about resource allocation, strategic direction, and investment. They need to understand what a project is, why it matters, and whether it is on track — all in a format that respects their limited time. They are not interested in technical details unless those details have direct business implications.

Common scenarios for executives using Code Atlas:

- **Evaluating a new project** for approval or funding.
- **Receiving a status update** on an ongoing project.
- **Conducting due diligence** before an acquisition or partnership.
- **Preparing board-level communication** about technical capabilities.

---

## Tone and Style

- **Concise and strategic**. Every sentence should justify its existence by conveying business value.
- **Outcome-focused**. Describe what the project achieves, not how it achieves it.
- **Confident and clear**. Avoid hedging language when the evidence supports a clear conclusion.
- **Structured for scanning**. Executives scan — use headers, bullet points, and bold text for key metrics.
- **No jargon**. If a technical term is used, its business implication must be immediately clear.

---

## Terminology

- Use business and strategic terminology: ROI, time-to-market, competitive advantage, scalability.
- Translate all technical concepts into business outcomes.
- Avoid acronyms unless they are widely understood in business contexts (API, SaaS, UX).
- Replace code-level terms with business equivalents: "microservices" becomes "modular architecture that enables independent scaling of features."

---

## Detail Level

- **Business value**: Full detail — purpose, market fit, and strategic positioning.
- **Project status**: Moderate detail — phase, momentum, and key risks.
- **Product capabilities**: Brief — what it does, not how it works.
- **Technology**: Minimal — name the stack but explain the business "why."
- **Architecture**: Minimal — one paragraph on the design approach.
- **Implementation details**: None — executives do not need to know about file structures or code patterns.

---

## What to Emphasize

- Business purpose and strategic value.
- Current status and trajectory.
- Key risks and their business impact.
- Competitive differentiation.
- Resource implications (team size, development phase).
- Time-to-completion indicators.

---

## What to De-emphasize

- Module-by-module technical breakdowns.
- Code quality details.
- Dependency lists.
- Build and deployment processes.
- Testing practices and coverage metrics.
- Individual file references.

---

## Recommended Analysis Levels

- **Quick**: Best for executive consumption — high-level summary without noise.
- **Standard**: Acceptable when the executive wants a bit more substance.
- **Deep**: Rarely appropriate — only for major strategic decisions like acquisitions.
